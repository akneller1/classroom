3Sale price

Multiply by what is left after the discount.
Example: $120 jacket, 30% off.
Multiplier: 1−0.30=0.701-0.30=0.701−0.30=0.70
Sale price: 0.70(120)=840.70(120)=840.70(120)=84
Answer: $84

4Stacked discounts

The second discount comes off the sale price.
Example: $100, 20% off, then an extra 10% off.
First: 0.80(100)=800.80(100)=800.80(100)=80
Second: 0.90(80)=720.90(80)=720.90(80)=72
Answer: $72 — a 28% total discount, not 30%

6Work backward

original=newmultiplier\text{original}=\dfrac{\text{new}}{\text{multiplier}}original=multipliernew​

2Markup and profit

Price = cost × (1 + markup rate); profit = price − cost.
Example: Cost $12, 60% markup, 50 sold.
Price: 12(1.60)=19.2012(1.60)=19.2012(1.60)=19.20
Profit: 50(19.20−12)=36050(19.20-12)=36050(19.20−12)=360
Answer: $19.20 each; $360 profit

5Membership break-even

A fee F that returns rate r of spending pays for itself at
S=FrS=\dfrac{F}{r}S=rF​
$60 fee, 5% back: 60÷0.05=1,20060\div0.05=1{,}20060÷0.05=1,200 of spending a year.
Below that, the membership costs more than it saves.

6Reminders & mistakes

✔ Do remember
  • Use one multiplier for each change.
  • Divide by the original for percent change.
✘ Common mistakes
  • 20% + 20% = 40% off: it is 36% off.
  • Undo +10% with −10%: divide by 1.10 instead.
  • Bigger box = better deal: check the unit price.
  • Tip on the after-tax total: tip on the pre-tax bill by custom.
