6Find amount financed

  • Start with out-the-door price: vehicle, tax, title and fees.
  • Subtract down payment and net trade-in equity.
  • If trade-in debt exceeds value, negative equity may be added to the new loan.
  • Compare financing with and without add-ons.
  • A $25,000 total price − $3,000 cash down gives $22,000 financed, assuming no trade-in or other financed costs.

5Compare complete offers

  • Same vehicle price and cash down?
  • Same fees, APR and payment timing?
  • Total of payments and any final balloon payment?
  • For a lease: upfront charges, mileage, wear and buyout terms?
  • How much will the car cost to operate?
  • A lower payment may fit today; ask whether the longer term creates negative-equity risk.

6Reminders & mistakes

✔ Do remember
  • Choose an affordable total cost, not only a payment.
  • Money factor × 2,400 approximates lease APR in percent.
✘ Common mistakes
  • APR always equals interest rate: fees may make APR higher.
  • Approval means affordable: test your own budget.
  • Interest stays constant: it follows the outstanding balance.
  • Lease payments build equity: ownership requires a separate purchase.
