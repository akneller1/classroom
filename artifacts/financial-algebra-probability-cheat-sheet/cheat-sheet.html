3Experimental estimate

Use the sample rate to predict.
Example: 12 defective of 400 tested; shipment of 5,000.
Rate: 12÷400=0.0312\div400=0.0312÷400=0.03

More practice: decide with expected value: games, warranties and lotteries

1Expected value

E=∑(outcome)(probability)E=\sum(\text{outcome})(\text{probability})E=∑(outcome)(probability) — include the cost as a negative.
$2 to play; win $10 on a 6:
16(10)−2≈−0.33\tfrac16(10)-2\approx-0.3361​(10)−2≈−0.33 per play.
A long-run average, not a guarantee.

2Fair price

Fair price = expected winnings (EV = 0).
Example: Win $24 if a 10-section spinner lands on 1 of 2 sections.
Expected winnings: 210(24)=4.80\tfrac{2}{10}(24)=4.80102​(24)=4.80
Answer: A fair price is $4.80

3Extended warranty

EV = P(repair) × repair cost − price.
Example: $129 plan; 10% chance of a $450 repair.
Expected repair: 0.10(450)=450.10(450)=450.10(450)=45
EV: 45−129=−8445-129=-8445−129=−84
Answer: −$84: costs more than it saves on average

4Lottery ticket

Add prize ÷ odds for each prize, then subtract the price.
Example: $2 ticket; $1,000 at 1 in 2,500; $4 at 1 in 20.
Prizes: 0.40+0.20=0.600.40+0.20=0.600.40+0.20=0.60
EV: 0.60−2=−1.400.60-2=-1.400.60−2=−1.40
Answer: −$1.40 per ticket

5Beyond expected value

  • Can I afford the worst case?
  • Do I need the money for sure (rent, tuition)?
  • Is a single play likely to match the average?
  • Independent events have no memory: no “due” wins.

6Reminders & mistakes

✔ Do remember
  • EV is a long-run average.
  • Count each outcome once.
✘ Common mistakes
  • A losing streak means a win is due: the gambler’s fallacy.
  • Add probabilities for “and”: multiply for independent events.
  • Positive EV = sure profit: single plays can still lose.
  • More tickets = better odds of profit: more tickets = bigger expected loss.
