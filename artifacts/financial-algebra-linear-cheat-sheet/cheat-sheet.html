4Goals with “at least”

6Check a linear model

  • Does x = 0 give the fee or starting value?
  • Does x = 1 add exactly one rate?
  • Are the units in the answer?
  • Is rounding up or down sensible for whole items?

1Break-even: two plans

Set the costs equal and solve.
Example: A: $20 + $8 per visit. B: $50 + $5 per visit.
Equal: 20+8x=50+5x20+8x=50+5x20+8x=50+5x
Solve: 3x=30⇒x=103x=30\Rightarrow x=103x=30⇒x=10
Answer: Same cost at 10 visits; B is cheaper above 10

2Break-even: a business

Each sale covers p−vp-vp−v of the fixed cost F.
Example: F = $1,800; v = $3.20; p = $9.
Per sale: 9−3.20=5.809-3.20=5.809−3.20=5.80
Units: 1,800÷5.80≈310.31{,}800\div5.80\approx310.31,800÷5.80≈310.3
Answer: 311 plates (round up)

3Profit

P=(p−v)q−FP=(p-v)q-FP=(p−v)q−F
q = 400, p = $9, v = $3.20, F = $1,800:
5.80(400)−1,800=5205.80(400)-1{,}800=5205.80(400)−1,800=520 → $520 profit
A negative P is a loss.

4Tiered electricity

Each rate applies only inside its tier.
Example: $12 fee; $0.12 to 500 kWh; $0.18 above; 700 kWh.
Tiers: 500(0.12)+200(0.18)=96500(0.12)+200(0.18)=96500(0.12)+200(0.18)=96
Bill: 12+96=10812+96=10812+96=108
Answer: $108

5“Or part of” and caps

Round partial units up; stop at any cap.
Example: $4 first hour, $2.50 each extra hour or part, $20 max; 3 h 10 min.
Billed hours: 4
Cost: 4+3(2.50)=11.504+3(2.50)=11.504+3(2.50)=11.50
Answer: $11.50 (under the $20 cap)

6Reminders & mistakes

✔ Do remember
  • Set models equal to compare plans.
  • Bill each tier at its own rate.
✘ Common mistakes
  • Lower fee = cheaper plan: only below the break-even.
  • F ÷ price for break-even: use F ÷ (price − variable cost).
  • All usage at the top tier: only usage above the threshold.
  • Round down for goals: round up to reach “at least.”
