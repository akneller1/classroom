5Simplified health cost

For covered in-network bills B, deductible d, coinsurance c and OOP cap M:
O=min⁡{M,min⁡(B,d)+cmax⁡(B−d,0)}O=\min\{M,\min(B,d)+c\max(B-d,0)\}O=min{M,min(B,d)+cmax(B−d,0)}
B, d, M, O are dollars; c is a decimal.
Assume no copays, all costs count toward deductible and cap, and no exclusions. Actual plans may apply separate service rules.
Annual total = 12 × monthly premium + O.

2Worst-case budget

For covered in-network care in the simplified plans:
Worst annual total=12p+M\text{Worst annual total}=12p+MWorst annual total=12p+M
p = monthly premium dollars; M = OOP cap.
A: $1,800 + $6,000 = $7,800

Noncovered or out-of-network costs may add more. A deductible is not the same as an OOP maximum.

4Deductible trade-off

Suppose raising a deductible from $500 to $1,500 saves $200 yearly.
Extra risk per qualifying claim = $1,000.
1,000200=5\dfrac{1{,}000}{200}=52001,000​=5 years of premium savings.
This is a simple recovery-time comparison, not a prediction of claims. Consider claim frequency and whether you could pay $1,500 tomorrow. A higher deductible can fit larger reserves.

5Compare the whole policy

  • Same covered risks, limits and exclusions?
  • Doctors, hospitals and medicines covered?
  • How much could I owe in a bad year?
  • Could I pay the deductible without new debt?
  • Are liability limits enough for the risk?
  • Prioritize losses you cannot comfortably absorb; smaller predictable costs may fit your own reserves.

6Reminders & mistakes

✔ Do remember
  • Premiums buy risk protection, even without a claim.
  • Compare both likely use and severe-loss scenarios.
✘ Common mistakes
  • Lowest premium = lowest cost: include your share of claims.
  • Deductible met = free care: coinsurance may remain.
  • OOP cap covers everything: premiums and exclusions may not count.
  • Expected cost = cash needed: a rare loss can be much larger.
