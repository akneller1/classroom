More practice: compare repayment and transfer choices

1Two repayment strategies

MethodHow it works
AvalanchePay minimums on all debts; send extra to highest APR first.
SnowballPay minimums on all; send extra to smallest balance first.
With equal total payments, fixed rates and no special fees, avalanche minimizes interest. Snowball may help motivation. Check promotional deadlines and penalties too.

2Which debt gets extra?

Both $1,000 debts remain for one month; minimums paid separately. A is 24% APR; B is 12%.

3Is a transfer better?

Owe $2,000; repay $200 principal monthly for 10 months. Interest uses each month’s opening principal; no new charges.

4Before transferring

  • Can you finish before the promotional rate ends?
  • What APR applies afterward?
  • Is the fee paid upfront or added to the debt?
  • Does a missed payment change the offer?
  • Is it 0% APR or deferred interest with different terms?
  • The transfer example favors 0% only because repayment finishes on time; behavior and contract terms matter.

5A practical card routine

  • Check the statement for unfamiliar charges.
  • Know the due date, APR and annual fee.
  • If possible, pay the full statement balance by the due date to preserve a purchase grace period.
  • Autopay at least the minimum can help avoid missed payments; keep enough cash in the account.
  • Stop new charges while paying down costly debt.

6Reminders & mistakes

✔ Do remember
  • Paying interest is not required to build credit.
  • Lower utilization can help; there is no guaranteed score cutoff.
✘ Common mistakes
  • APR used as monthly rate: convert annual to monthly.
  • Minimum means recommended: it is only a requirement.
  • 0% means no cost: check transfer fees and deadlines.
  • Rewards offset all costs: interest and fees can exceed rewards.
