1Which pathway pays back?

Certificate costs $12,000 and adds $6,000 yearly earnings. Degree costs $40,000 and adds $10,000. Same baseline; ignore tax, raises and financing.
Example: Simple payback = cost ÷ annual earnings gain.
Certificate: 12,0006,000=2\dfrac{12{,}000}{6{,}000}=26,00012,000​=2 years
Degree: 40,00010,000=4\dfrac{40{,}000}{10{,}000}=410,00040,000​=4 years
Answer: Certificate pays back sooner

2Sooner vs. larger return

The certificate wins on speed in that model. Over 10 working years after completion:
Certificate: $60,000 gain − $12,000 = $48,000.
Degree: $100,000 gain − $40,000 = $60,000.
The degree has a larger modeled 10-year gain. Different completion times, job placement, missed earnings or personal goals can reverse the choice.

3Count opportunity cost

Economic education cost = net price + earnings forgone + financing costs, using a consistent baseline.
Two years with $20,000 yearly earnings forgone plus $15,000 total net price:

Subtract earnings while studying. Count only living-cost differences if baseline costs are already included.

5Before committing

  • Compare net price across all years, not only year one.
  • Check completion, transfer-credit and job-placement outcomes for the actual program.
  • Estimate realistic local earnings, not only a national high salary.
  • Compare apprenticeship, certificate, transfer and direct-to-work routes.
  • Model affordable payments using expected take-home pay.
  • Check whether aid requires a GPA, enrollment level or annual application.

6Reminders & mistakes

✔ Do remember
  • Use multiple earnings and completion scenarios.
  • Loan payments compete with rent and other needs.
✘ Common mistakes
  • Loans are a discount: they finance the price.
  • Starting salary decides ROI: include time, costs and risk.
  • Borrowing below salary is always safe: test a full budget.
  • Sticker price = your cost: compare gift aid and net price.
