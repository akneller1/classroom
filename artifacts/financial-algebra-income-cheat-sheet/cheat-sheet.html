3Hourly pay & overtime

For this example, hours above 40 in one workweek earn 1.5 times the regular wage. Eligibility varies.

6Read a paycheck

Deductions are fictional. Payroll tax uses the course model: 6.2% Social Security + 1.45% Medicare = 7.65% of wages shown; ignore wage caps.
Example: $2,000 gross per biweekly paycheck.
Taxes: $150 federal + $153 payroll + $90 state
Other: $60 health + $100 retirement
Total deducted: 150+153+90+60+100=553150+153+90+60+100=553150+153+90+60+100=553
Answer: $1,447 net per check

3Tax: add the slices

Example: Taxable income = $39,000.
First slice: 0.10(12,000)=1,2000.10(12{,}000)=1{,}2000.10(12,000)=1,200

4Marginal vs. effective

Marginal: rate on the next taxable dollar: 12% in the example.
Effective: average tax rate; state the denominator.
On gross: 4,44054,000×100%≈8.22%\dfrac{4{,}440}{54{,}000}\times100\%\approx8.22\%54,0004,440​×100%≈8.22%
On taxable: 4,44039,000×100%≈11.38%\dfrac{4{,}440}{39{,}000}\times100\%\approx11.38\%39,0004,440​×100%≈11.38%
Only the income in a higher slice gets its higher rate.

5Which job is better?

Assume equal hours, taxes ignored, equal other benefits; full employer match earned and vested.
Example: A: $52,000 + 5% match; $80 monthly health premium. B: $56,000; no match; $350 premium.
A value: 52,000+2,600−960=53,64052{,}000+2{,}600-960=53{,}64052,000+2,600−960=53,640
B value: 56,000−4,200=51,80056{,}000-4{,}200=51{,}80056,000−4,200=51,800
Answer: A adds $1,840 of annual value

6Reminders & mistakes

✔ Do remember
  • Compare commute, leave, schedule and growth too; B offers more cash before premiums.
  • Employer match is retirement value, not take-home pay.
✘ Common mistakes
  • “Higher bracket taxes all pay”: only the slice changes.
  • Budgeting on gross: use net; benefits and taxes vary.
  • Guaranteed overtime: do not assume it in a basic budget.
