2The model

y=a(1+r)ty=a(1+r)^ty=a(1+r)t growth  ·  y=a(1−r)ty=a(1-r)^ty=a(1−r)t decay
a = start, r = rate per period (decimal), t = periods.
V=1,100(0.80)tV=1{,}100(0.80)^tV=1,100(0.80)t: a $1,100 laptop losing 20% a year.

3Growth

Multiply by the factor once per period.
Example: $2,000 at 5% a year for 10 years.
Model: 2,000(1.05)102{,}000(1.05)^{10}2,000(1.05)10

4Depreciation

Keep 1 − r of the value each year.
Example: $24,000 car, 15% a year, 3 years.
Model: 24,000(0.85)324{,}000(0.85)^324,000(0.85)3
Value: ≈14,739\approx14{,}739≈14,739
Answer: About $14,739

5Rate from two values

factor = new ÷ old; rate = |factor − 1|.
Example: Car: $30,000 → $25,800 in one year.
Factor: 25,800÷30,000=0.8625{,}800\div30{,}000=0.8625,800÷30,000=0.86
Rate: 1−0.86=0.141-0.86=0.141−0.86=0.14
Answer: 14% per year

6Read a model

  • y = 3,200(1.045)ᵗ → starts at 3,200 and grows 4.5% per period.
  • y = 650(0.88)ᵗ → starts at 650 and decays 12% per period.
  • b > 1 grows; 0 < b < 1 decays.

4Half-life

Count half-lives, then halve that many times.
Example: 320 mg, half-life 6 hours, after 18 hours.
Half-lives: 18÷6=318\div6=318÷6=3

6Reminders & mistakes

✔ Do remember
  • Factor = 1 ± rate, not the rate.
  • Count periods carefully.
✘ Common mistakes
  • 10% twice = 20%: it is 21% (1.1² = 1.21).
  • Decay reaches $0: it only gets closer to 0.
  • Subtract r% of the original each year: that is linear, not exponential.
  • Rule of 72 is exact: it is an estimate.
