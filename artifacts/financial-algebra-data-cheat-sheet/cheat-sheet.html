1Data words

TermMeaning / quick example
MeanSum ÷ count.
MedianMiddle value in order.
OutlierA value far from the rest.

2Mean or median?

Outliers pull the mean; the median resists.
Example: Salaries $42k, $45k, $48k, $51k, $350k.
Median: $48,000
Mean: 536,000÷5=107,200536{,}000\div5=107{,}200536,000÷5=107,200
Answer: The median ($48,000) is more typical

3IQR

Order the data; Q1 and Q3 are the medians of the lower and upper halves.
Example: 12, 15, 18, 20, 24, 27, 31, 40
Q1, Q3: Q1=16.5, Q3=29Q_1=16.5,\ Q_3=29Q1​=16.5, Q3​=29
IQR: 29−16.5=12.529-16.5=12.529−16.5=12.5
Answer: 12.5

4Score needed

Target total = target mean × number of scores.
Example: 78, 85, 90; want a mean of 85 after 4.
Target total: 85(4)=34085(4)=34085(4)=340
Needed: 340−253=87340-253=87340−253=87
Answer: 87

5Reading r

rDescribe as
−0.86strong negative
0.52moderate positive
0.12weak positive
Strong |r| ≥ 0.7; moderate 0.4–0.7; weak below 0.4. Sign gives direction.

6Correlation is not causation

  • Look for a lurking variable (heat → ice cream and sunburns).
  • Ask whether the cause could run the other way.
  • Only a randomized experiment shows cause and effect.

More practice: predict with lines of best fit, residuals and honest graphs

1Line of best fit

y^=mx+b\hat y=mx+by^​=mx+b — the hat means “predicted.”
Slope m: predicted change in y for each 1 unit of x.
y^=−145x+26,500\hat y=-145x+26{,}500y^​=−145x+26,500 (price vs. thousands of miles):
each 1,000 miles → $145 lower predicted price.

2Predict and solve

Substitute x to predict; set ŷ to a value to solve for x.
Example: y^=−145x+26,500\hat y=-145x+26{,}500y^​=−145x+26,500
x = 60: −145(60)+26,500=17,800-145(60)+26{,}500=17{,}800−145(60)+26,500=17,800
ŷ = $20,700: x=(20,700−26,500)÷(−145)=40x=(20{,}700-26{,}500)\div(-145)=40x=(20,700−26,500)÷(−145)=40
Answer: $17,800; 40 thousand miles

3Residual

residual=actual−predicted\text{residual}=\text{actual}-\text{predicted}residual=actual−predicted
Example: Predicted $17,800; listed $16,200.
Residual: 16,200−17,800=−1,60016{,}200-17{,}800=-1{,}60016,200−17,800=−1,600
Meaning: below the line
Answer: −$1,600: priced below the model

4Truncated axis

Compare what the bars show with the real difference.
Example: Ratings 52 and 58; axis starts at 50.
Looks: (58−50)÷(52−50)=4(58-50)\div(52-50)=4(58−50)÷(52−50)=4 times as tall
Actually: (58−52)÷52≈11.5%(58-52)\div52\approx11.5\%(58−52)÷52≈11.5% higher
Answer: A modest gap looks huge

5Trust a model?

  • Is x inside the range of the data? (no extrapolation)
  • Do residuals look random, not curved?
  • Is the sample large and fair?
  • Do predictions make sense (no scores above 100)?

6Reminders & mistakes

✔ Do remember
  • Sort before finding a median.
  • Residual = actual − predicted.
✘ Common mistakes
  • Strong r means causation: look for a lurking variable.
  • Negative r is weak: the sign is direction, |r| is strength.
  • Predict far outside the data: extrapolation is risky.
  • Bars may start anywhere: bar graphs should start at 0.
