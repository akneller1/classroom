Quick start

Most visits follow the same three steps.

  1. Pick a courseUse the course names across the top, or a subject tile on the home page.
  2. Open a unitEach unit page has everything for that unit: generators, games and study guides.
  3. Create or playMake a lesson, worksheet, activity or assessment, or open a game or study guide.

Sharing with students

Give students a link to one activity, without the rest of the site.

How do I send students to one game or study guide?
  1. Find the activity on its unit page or in Browse Activities.
  2. Click Copy student link on its card.
  3. For most games, a window opens first. Choose the skills students should practice, then copy the link.
  4. Paste the link into Google Classroom, Canvas, Teams or an email.

The student link opens only that activity, with only the skills you chose. Students don't see the site's menus, course pages, generators or answer keys.

Which activities have a student link?

Activities that students can do on their own:

  • Flashcards
  • Picture Reveal
  • Detective
  • Escape Room
  • Slope Links Mini Golf
  • Zombie Fence
  • Cheat Sheets
  • Interactive Math tools
  • The Assessment Readiness Check

Whole-class games (Showdown, Connect 4, Tic-Tac-Toe and Math Millionaire) don't have one, because they are meant to be run from the teacher's screen. Printables don't either: print them or share the PDF.

How do I give students the Assessment Readiness Check?

On the unit page, use Copy student link on the Assessment Readiness Check card. A setup window opens first. In it you choose:

  • level
  • number of questions (5–25)
  • time limit (none, 20, 30 or 45 minutes)
  • answer format (mixed, multiple choice, or short answer graded automatically)
  • skills

Students take it online and get worked solutions and skill-by-skill feedback right away. Then they can practice what they missed with new questions.

Can students get from a student link to the rest of the site?

Not by clicking: a student link has no menus or links back to the site. It is a focused view, though, not a password. A student who edits the web address by hand could still reach the public pages.

Answer keys only appear when you build or print them from a generator. They are never part of a student link. Keep printed keys and downloaded key PDFs out of shared folders.

A shared tool keeps the course name of the course it was built for, and its worksheet is the same in every course.

What's in each tool?
  • The tool: sliders, draggable points and models, such as the Slope-Intercept Explorer, the Triangle Congruence Builder and the Unit Circle to Sine Wave.
  • A "Try this" list: five or so challenges that check themselves. Each one turns green when a student does it.
  • An Explore & Reflect worksheet: a two-page printable PDF, with a teacher key, linked on the tool's card. The keys for the 6th–8th grade, Precalculus and Calculus tools end with teacher notes: the intended discovery, likely misconceptions and which answers can vary.
  • A “What should I notice?” panel (6th–8th grade, Precalculus and Calculus tools): closed until a student opens it, so it doesn't give the discovery away.
How do I run a lesson with one?
  1. Print the Explore & Reflect worksheet from the tool's card.
  2. Predict (Part 1): students answer before they open the tool. Wrong predictions are fine; they make the next step stick.
  3. Explore (Part 2): students open the tool with the student link, or you project it, and fill in the tables.
  4. Write the rule (Part 3): students fill in sentence frames that state what they discovered, for example "Changing b moves the line up or down."
  5. Check and Reflect (Parts 4–5): students answer without the tool, as a quick check that the rule transferred.

The 6th–8th grade, Precalculus and Calculus worksheets use the same idea with these parts: Predict (before the tool), Explore (guided tasks with the tool), Record (a table), Explain (sentence frames and one written explanation) and Apply (a new situation without the tool).

Part 2 usually takes 15–20 minutes and the whole worksheet fits in a class period. The "Try this" list works well as a warm-up or for early finishers.

Can students use them on their own devices?

Yes. Each tool has a Copy student link button and works on Chromebooks, laptops, tablets and phones. Points can be dragged with a mouse, a finger, or the arrow keys. Nothing is saved or submitted: the worksheet is the record of their work.

Projecting a tool for the whole class? Have students call out predictions before you move a slider, then reveal. The tools also work in dark mode.

Making resources

Four generators sit at the top of every unit page and on the home page. All of them share a few options.

Options every generator shares
  • Level. CP2, CP1 or Honors in high school; Remedial, Standard or Advanced in 6th–8th grade. The level sets the difficulty of every problem.
  • Skills. Tick the skills from the unit you want. Skills that don't suit a format are greyed out, with the reason shown.
  • Versions. Up to three versions (A, B, C) with different numbers, which helps against copying.
  • Student copy and answer key. Switch between the two views, and print each separately.
  • New problems. Regenerates the problems with the same settings.

Answers to generated problems are checked by the site. If you ever spot a mistake, use Feedback or report an issue at the bottom of any page.

This is a full day's lesson from the course's scope and sequence:

  • objective
  • vocabulary
  • Do Now
  • notes
  • worked examples
  • practice
  • exit ticket
  • Course, unit and day. Choose them at the top. On Lesson days you can tick other lessons in the unit to combine their problems.
  • Student notes. Completed notes fill everything in; Guided notes leave key words and answers blank. The teacher key always prints completed.
  • Include in lesson. Untick sections to leave them out. You can edit section minutes and notes; your edits are saved in your browser.

Worksheet
  • What it makes. Practice worksheets for any unit, mixing multiple-choice and show-your-work problems.
  • Settings. Choose skills, level and number of versions.
  • Answer key. It includes worked solutions.
Activity (printables and projects)

Pick a printable on the left, then a level and skills:

  • Maze: each correct answer leads to the next room.
  • Color by Number: answers map to colors that reveal a picture.
  • Tarsia Puzzle: cut out triangles and match problems to answers.
  • Error Analysis: find, explain and fix a student's mistake.
  • Partner Check: Partner A and Partner B solve different problems with the same answers.
  • Whodunnit: clue cards to post around the room, and a case board to solve.
  • Stations: one page per station, plus an answer key.
  • Matching Card Sort: function units only. Match graph, equation, table and description cards; Play online opens the digital version.
  • Vocabulary Builder: matching, definition, diagram and crossword sheets.
  • Projects: 2–3 day projects in CP2, CP1 and Honors versions. Download them as PDF, Word or PowerPoint, with a teacher guide.
Assessment
  • Formats. Build a standard unit test (multiple choice and short answer) or a choice-based menu assessment, where students pick problems to reach a point total.
  • Settings. Choose skills, level, number of versions and point values.
  • Editing. On the review screen you can replace, move, delete or edit any problem, or write your own.
  • Your own problems. The site can't check an answer it didn't generate. Tick I checked this answer on the problem card to approve it before printing.

Saved assessments stay in your browser, and you can download one as a file to keep or move to another computer.

Finding things

Ways to get around
  • Search (top right) accepts a topic ("slope"), a format ("escape room") or both.
  • Unit pages have Previous unit and Next unit buttons, and the Back button returns you to where you were.
  • Browse Activities (the Games and Study Guides tiles) can be filtered by subject, unit, category and activity type.
  • Save on any card adds it to Saved activities for quick access later.

FAQ

Do students or teachers need an account?

No. Nothing to sign up for, and nothing about students is collected.

Where are my saved activities and edits stored?

In the browser you used: saved activities, lesson-plan edits and saved assessments. They won't follow you to another computer or browser, and clearing browsing data removes them. To move an assessment, download it as a file and open it on the other computer.

Why does a game ask me to choose skills when I open it?

So one game can fit a whole unit or just today's lesson. Choose the skills and level, then start. A student link remembers the skills you picked.

Something printed badly or an answer looks wrong. What do I do?

For printing, use the generator's own Print buttons. They set the page size and margins, so turn off "Fit to page" scaling in the print dialog.

For a wrong answer, use Feedback or report an issue at the bottom of any page. Include the link and the problem, and it will be fixed.

Which browsers work?

Any current version of Chrome, Edge, Safari or Firefox, on computers, Chromebooks and tablets. Phones work for flashcards and review games; the generators are easier on a larger screen.
